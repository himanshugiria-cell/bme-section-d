1. A motor creates motion

An electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical motion. In a rotary motor, the stator stays still, the rotor turns, and the shaft delivers rotation to a wheel, joint or other mechanism. A motor is an actuator. It is not the sensor that measures force or detects an object.

Simple permanent-magnet brushed DC motor

Figure 1. Simplified construction; current and force directions are omitted. The picture explains the parts and is not a wiring or assembly drawing.

Working, in order: The DC supply sends current through the brushes and commutator into the armature winding. A current-carrying conductor in the stator magnetic field experiences force. Forces on the coil produce torque, a turning effect. As the rotor turns, the commutator switches winding current at appropriate positions so the turning effect continues in the same rotational direction. TI: brushed DC working.

Control: Suitable supply voltage or a driver can adjust speed; the load also affects speed. A driver may use PWM to vary average electrical input. For this permanent-magnet brushed example, reversing terminal polarity reverses rotation. A basic motor connected to a suitable DC supply tends to rotate continuously; it does not know a desired stopping angle by itself.

Advantages and limits: This construction is simple and useful for small continuous-drive tasks, such as a toy wheel. Brushes wear, and precise speed or position needs suitable control. Not every DC motor has brushes: a brushless motor uses electronic commutation. maxon: control and feedback.

आसान भाषा में: Motor गति देता है। किसी तय angle पर रोकने के लिए सही control और position की जानकारी चाहिए।

2. A stepper follows angular commands

A stepper motor has stator windings and a rotor. Its driver energises winding phases in a controlled sequence. The magnetic interaction makes the rotor follow angular increments called steps. A suitable driver is needed; a constant battery connection to one winding does not produce the intended stepping sequence.

Figure 2. Conceptual command sequence. The real step angle depends on the motor and driver mode; this is not the construction of a particular 1.8° motor.

In a specified full-step mode, command count requests angular displacement; command rate requests speed; phase sequence/direction control sets rotation direction. Repeated steps can produce complete revolutions. At a sufficiently high rate the motion can look smooth. Examples include positioning stages and some 3D-printer or small CNC mechanisms. These machines may also use other motor arrangements. NPTEL: stepper.

Positioning needs a reference: Suppose the starting position is known as 0°. Counting steps gives the expected position relative to that reference. Many systems establish a starting position using a home/reference sensor.

Commanded is not automatically achieved: Too much load, speed or acceleration can cause missed steps. An open-loop controller does not directly measure that mistake. Energised windings can hold the shaft, but holding torque is finite. Closed-loop stepper systems also exist. Oriental Motor: stepper control.

आसान भाषा में: Driver coils को क्रम से चालू करता है। Shaft steps में घूमता है। Command गिनने से expected position मिलती है; असली position की जाँच अलग बात है।

Servo is a control arrangement. A position servo compares target position with measured position and acts to reduce the difference. The underlying motor can be DC or AC. Servo systems may also control speed or torque; “servo” does not universally mean a hobby motor restricted to 180°. NPTEL: servo.

Worked learning examples

A. Stepper angle: Full-step angle = 1.8°, known starting position, no missed steps. One revolution = 360 ÷ 1.8 = 200 steps. A 90° move requires 90 ÷ 1.8 = 50 step commands. The result is commanded displacement under these assumptions.

B. Stepper speed: In the same configuration, 400 commands/s ÷ 200 steps/revolution = 2 revolutions/s. Multiply by 60: 120 rpm, if the rotor remains synchronised.

C. Continuous DC rotation: A measured steady speed of 120 rpm means 2 revolutions/s. In 15 s, the shaft makes 30 revolutions. This does not by itself guarantee a chosen final stopping angle.

D. Servo error: Target = 60°, measured position = 52°. With error defined as target minus measured value, error = +8°. The controller commands correction; it does not make all error disappear instantly.

Misconceptions to avoid

A DC motor can position with suitable feedback and control. A stepper can rotate continuously through repeated steps. DC PWM pulses regulate electrical input; they do not each mean one shaft step. Motor, sensor and end effector describe different roles, even when one assembly contains all three.

4. Control: a command is not a measurement

A control system makes a process or machine behave in a desired way. The input/reference/setpoint is the requested result; the output is the result produced. The controller decides the action; a drive/actuator applies it to the physical system, often called the plant.

Figure 3. A position servo is the closed-loop example. Arrows carrying information are distinct from the physical motion produced by the motor.

Open-loopClosed-loop
No output measurement is fed back for correction.Measured output is fed back and compared with the target.
Simple and economical for suitable tasks.Can correct disturbances or position error within its limits.
Errors from load changes may remain uncorrected.Needs sensing and suitable controller design; complexity/cost increase.
Example: a timed motor run without position sensing.Example: a joint moved towards a target using an angle sensor.

Worked situation: A DC wheel drive receives the same voltage on a slope and on level ground. The extra load may reduce speed. In open-loop operation, no speed measurement triggers a correction. In a suitable closed-loop speed system, a speed sensor reports the change and the controller adjusts the drive. Feedback helps, but cannot overcome every physical limit. maxon: controller benefit.

आसान भाषा में: Open-loop में केवल आदेश दिया जाता है। Closed-loop में परिणाम मापा जाता है और जरूरत हो तो सुधार किया जाता है।

5. Robotics: identify each component's job

Automation uses a control arrangement to perform a task with reduced continuous human intervention. A robot is a programmable mechanism that can perform motions and tasks. A simple automatic temperature controller is automation without being a robot.

ComponentJobIntroductory example
SensorDetects or measures an input/output conditionProximity sensor detects a box; angle sensor measures a joint
ControllerUses inputs and instructions to decide actionDecides when to move and what position to request
ActuatorConverts supplied energy into motion or forceElectric motor turns; cylinder extends
End effectorTool/device at the robot's working end that acts on the objectGripper holds; suction cup lifts; welding tool joins

Actuators: motion need not be rotation

Actuation typeEnergy/working mediumMotion example
ElectricElectrical energyMotor turns a shaft; a solenoid can move a plunger
HydraulicPressurised liquidHydraulic cylinder pushes or lifts
PneumaticCompressed airPneumatic cylinder opens a gripper

Hydraulic = liquid; pneumatic = air. A cylinder usually provides linear motion; a rotary motor provides angular motion. A gripper may contain a pneumatic actuator, but “gripper” names its task at the robot's end, while “pneumatic” describes how it is actuated. NPTEL: electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic drives.

Sensors give information: A proximity sensor detects presence without necessarily touching; a position sensor reports location/angle; a force sensor measures force; a temperature sensor measures temperature. The motor does not become the force sensor because it drives the gripper.

Follow one pick-and-place action

  1. A proximity sensor detects a box.
  2. The controller checks the task instructions and commands the drive.
  3. Motor/actuator motion carries the gripper to the box.
  4. The end effector grips or lifts it and releases it at the destination.
  5. Position/force feedback, when fitted, helps check and control the motion or grip.

Sensor जानकारी देता है; controller आदेश देता है; actuator गति देता है; end effector वस्तु पर काम करता है। This is a mechatronic system because mechanics, electrical parts, sensing and control work together.

6. Materials: groups and properties are different

The four broad groups below are a useful introductory classification. Their usual properties are tendencies, not universal rules. Material choice depends on the actual grade, processing and task.

GroupExamplesTypical useful feature and caution

PolymersPolyethylene, nylonOften light and easy to shape; heat/load resistance varies
CeramicsAlumina, porcelainOften hard and heat-resistant; many are brittle
CompositesGlass-fibre polymer, reinforced concreteCombine constituents for desired properties; direction/structure matter

Properties you should be able to distinguish

PropertyMeaningRecognition example
StrengthAbility to withstand loading without the specified yielding/failureShaft must carry its working load
StiffnessResistance to elastic deformationArm should bend very little
HardnessResistance to local indentation/scratchingSurface resists a dent
ToughnessAbility to absorb energy before fracturePart survives impact without breaking
BrittlenessTendency to fracture with little plastic deformationTypical glass breaks without much permanent bending
DuctilityAbility to plastically deform in tension before fractureCopper can be drawn into wire
MalleabilityAbility to plastically deform under compressionMetal can be shaped into sheet

Stiffness and strength are different. A part can deform too much while still being below its failure load. Hardness and toughness are also different: a hard material may still break under impact. Shape and size influence a component's stiffness as well as the material's elastic behaviour.

Other properties from the original notes remain important: wear resistance, corrosion resistance, thermal conductivity and electrical conductivity. A wire requires electrical conductivity; a heat-transfer part benefits from thermal conductivity. Suitability involves more than one property. For the mechanical-property definitions above, see NPTEL: elastic behaviour and strength, NPTEL: hardness and toughness, and Penn State: ductility, malleability and brittle fracture.

Worked choice: A robot arm must deflect very little: consider stiffness. Its surface must resist indentation: consider hardness. It must survive a sudden impact: consider toughness. None of these statements alone supplies a complete engineering design.

7. Assignment 1 support: heat treatment

Scope label: Heat treatment is asked in Assignment 1, Question 8. It is not a separately named topic in the official Unit I content. This short explanation supports the assignment at an introductory level.

Heat treatment deliberately heats, holds and cools a metal/alloy in the solid state to obtain desired structure/properties. It generally changes properties rather than deliberately changing the part's shape.

Figure 4. Qualitative time–temperature sequence. This is not a temperature schedule for any specified alloy.

Heating raises the metal to the selected temperature. Holding/soaking gives time for the intended changes and temperature distribution. Controlled cooling follows a chosen route; cooling rate affects the result.

Introductory names: annealing can soften and improve ductility; normalising is commonly used for suitable steels to refine/regularise structure; hardening of suitable steels uses an appropriate heating and quenching sequence; tempering after hardening reduces brittleness and adjusts the hardness–toughness balance. The outcome depends on the alloy and process, so do not apply one schedule to every metal. NPTEL: heat-treatment introduction, NPTEL: tempering.

8. Revision quiz Q5: checked clarification

Question: Which statement correctly matches a motor or actuator with its function or working medium?

Correct option A: “A servo motor provides controlled position; a hydraulic actuator uses pressurised liquid.” This is also the selected key in the live Google Form checked on 9 October 2026.

Why the other options fail: a stepper produces angular motion, rather than detecting nearby objects; a DC motor produces motion, rather than measuring grip force; pneumatic actuation uses compressed air, rather than liquid or molten metal; an end effector is the working tool, not automatically a servo motor. The stepper and DC terms appear in Q5's distractors, so understanding their roles helps eliminate these choices.

9. Revision questions with answers

Try an answer aloud or on paper before checking below. These are learning checks, not promised examination questions.

1. Name the parts in the brushed DC motor and explain why it keeps turning. Fixed stator magnets provide a field; current enters the rotating armature through brushes and commutator; magnetic force produces torque. The commutator switches winding current as the rotor turns so rotation continues.

2. Does every DC motor have brushes? Can a DC motor position a joint? No: brushless motors use electronic commutation. Yes, with suitable position sensing, drive and control.

3. What sets expected stepper displacement and speed? In the specified step mode, command count sets displacement and command rate sets speed. Direction control/phase sequence sets direction.

4. A 1.8° full-step motor is commanded through 45°. How many steps? 45 ÷ 1.8 = 25 commands, assuming a known start and no missed steps. Without those conditions this does not prove actual position.

5. Why might an open-loop stepper be in the wrong place? Missed steps from excessive load/speed/acceleration, or an unknown/lost starting reference. A controller's command count alone does not measure shaft position.

6. Distinguish a servo from a basic motor connected to a supply. A position servo uses controller/drive and measured position feedback to reduce target-minus-actual error; the basic supply-only motor lacks this arrangement.

7. Target angle is 80°; measured angle is 73°. What is the error? With target minus actual as the convention, +7°. A suitable controller acts to reduce it.

8. Match detect, decide, move and hold to components. Sensor detects; controller decides; actuator moves; gripper/end effector holds.

9. What working media distinguish hydraulic and pneumatic actuation? Pressurised liquid and compressed air, respectively.

10. Is an automatic thermostat necessarily a robot? No. It performs automatic control without necessarily being a programmable moving robot mechanism.

11. Which properties describe little bending, dent resistance and impact-energy absorption? Stiffness, hardness and toughness, respectively. Brittleness means little plastic deformation before fracture.

12. What are the three main heat-treatment stages? Heating, holding/soaking and controlled cooling. This supports Assignment 1; no numerical alloy schedule is specified here.
