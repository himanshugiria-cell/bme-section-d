Part A: syllabus-level study

1. What a pump does

A pump adds mechanical energy to a liquid. This helps the liquid move through pipes, rise to a higher level, or overcome pressure and friction losses. Examples include water supply, irrigation and cooling-water circulation.

FeatureReciprocating pumpCentrifugal pump
Main moving partPiston or plunger moving back and forthRotating impeller with vanes
How liquid receives energyChanging cylinder volume and pressureRotating vanes transfer energy to liquid
DeliveryPulsating, stroke by strokeGenerally smoother and continuous
Typical dutyControlled small flow at relatively high pressureWater supply, irrigation or circulation at a required flow and head

2. Reciprocating pump: suction and delivery

A crank and connecting rod convert rotation into the back-and-forth motion of a piston. One-way valves make liquid enter through the suction side and leave through the delivery side.

  • Suction stroke: piston moves away from the valve end; cylinder volume increases; pressure falls; suction valve opens; liquid enters.
  • Delivery stroke: piston moves towards the valve end; cylinder volume decreases; pressure rises; delivery valve opens; liquid leaves.

आसान भाषा में: जगह बढ़ती है तो pressure घटता है और liquid अंदर आता है। जगह घटती है तो pressure बढ़ता है और liquid बाहर जाता है।

3. Centrifugal pump and priming

In a centrifugal pump, liquid enters at the eye near the centre of the impeller. The rotating impeller vanes transfer energy to the liquid, moving it outward. The casing collects and guides it into the delivery pipe.

Priming means filling an ordinary non-self-priming centrifugal pump casing and suction line with liquid and removing air before starting. An air-filled pump may rotate without producing the pressure difference needed to draw water in.

4. Pump efficiency: what the ratios mean

Efficiency compares useful output with input. It is a ratio or percentage, not a separate kind of power. Discharge (Q) means the liquid volume delivered per unit time (for example, L/min or m³/s). Head (H) means the energy given to each unit weight of liquid, commonly expressed as an equivalent height in metres.

QuantityMeaningUseful formula
Discharge, QLiquid volume delivered per unit timeUse L/min or m³/s consistently
Head, HEnergy supplied to liquid per unit weightUsually stated in metres
Theoretical discharge, single actingIdeal liquid volume delivered each secondQth = A × L × N / 60
Volumetric efficiencyActual flow compared with theoretical flowηv = Qactual / Qth × 100%
Overall pump efficiencyUseful liquid power compared with shaft inputηo = ρgQH / Pshaft × 100%

Symbols and units: A = piston area (m²), L = stroke length (m), N = crank speed (revolutions/minute), ρ = liquid density (kg/m³), and g ≈ 9.81 m/s². In the overall-efficiency formula, use Q in m³/s, H in metres and shaft input power Pshaft in watts.

Worked idea: If the theoretical discharge is 60 L/min and actual discharge is 54 L/min, ηv = 54/60 × 100 = 90%. Keep the flow units the same before dividing.

5. Refrigeration: moving heat

Refrigeration removes heat from a cold space and rejects it to warmer surroundings. It does not destroy heat. A refrigerant is the working fluid that carries heat through the system.

ComponentWhat it doesRefrigerant condition after it
EvaporatorAbsorbs heat from the cold spaceLow-pressure vapour
CompressorRaises vapour pressure and temperature using work inputHigh-pressure vapour
CondenserRejects heat to surroundings and condenses vapourHigh-pressure liquid
Expansion deviceReduces pressure before the evaporatorLow-pressure liquid-vapour mixture

6. Refrigerant properties and refrigeration-system types

A suitable refrigerant should evaporate and condense at useful conditions, transfer heat effectively, remain stable and compatible with equipment, and have acceptable safety and environmental properties. Safety includes low toxicity and manageable flammability; environmental considerations include low ozone-depletion and global-warming impact.

System typeMain idea
Vapour compressionA mechanical compressor handles refrigerant vapour; common in refrigerators and room AC systems.
Vapour absorptionAn absorbent, solution pump and heat-driven generator replace the main mechanical compressor role.
Air cycleAir is compressed, cooled and expanded; its temperature falls during expansion.
ThermoelectricElectric current produces cooling through the Peltier effect; useful in small applications.

COP: COP = cooling rate / input power. If a refrigerator removes 2.0 kW of heat with 0.5 kW input, COP = 2.0 / 0.5 = 4. A COP greater than one is possible because the system moves heat; it does not create energy.

7. Air conditioning

Air conditioning maintains desired indoor conditions. These can include temperature, humidity, cleanliness and air movement. A room AC usually cools and circulates indoor air; moisture may condense on the cold coil and leave through a drain.

आसान भाषा में: AC कमरे की गर्मी refrigerant के द्वारा बाहर छोड़ता है। Drain में निकलने वाला पानी हवा की नमी से बनता है; वह refrigerant नहीं है।

Classification basisTypesMeaning
PurposeComfort / industrialFor people’s comfort or for a process/material needing controlled conditions
Seasonal dutySummer / winter / year-roundCooling, heating and humidity treatment as needed
ArrangementWindow / split / centralHow equipment is arranged and serves the space
FeatureWindow ACSplit AC
ArrangementMain parts in one casingIndoor and outdoor units are separate

The basic cooling principle is the same in both: evaporator absorbs room heat; compressor raises pressure; condenser rejects heat outdoors; expansion device lowers pressure before the evaporator. A ductless split AC circulates room air. Air circulation and fresh-air ventilation are different functions.

Part B: Assignment 3 support

These points support questions in Assignment 3. They are useful extensions of the Unit III teaching; they are not additional official syllabus headings.

Double-acting reciprocating pump

In a double-acting pump, both sides of the piston handle liquid. While one side fills, the other side delivers. On the next stroke their roles exchange. The exact discharge is affected by the piston-rod area, so do not simply state that it is always exactly double that of a single-acting pump.

One ton of refrigeration (TR)

1 TR is a rate of cooling, not the mass of a machine. It is conventionally the heat-removal rate needed to freeze one short ton of water (2,000 lb, approximately 907 kg) at 0°C to ice at 0°C in 24 hours. 1 TR ≈ 3.517 kW (approximately 211 kJ/min).

Revision check

  1. Why does a reciprocating pump need suction and delivery valves?
  2. Why is priming needed before starting an ordinary centrifugal pump?
  3. Which efficiency compares actual discharge with theoretical discharge?
  4. Trace the refrigerant from the evaporator to the compressor, condenser and expansion device.
  5. Why can refrigeration COP be greater than one?
  6. List two refrigerant properties besides cooling performance.
  7. What is the difference between air circulation and ventilation?
  8. State two arrangement differences between a window AC and a split AC.

Self-check answers: valves control one-way flow; priming removes air and fills the liquid path; volumetric efficiency; low-pressure vapour → high-pressure vapour → high-pressure liquid → low-pressure mixture; COP measures heat moved per input; safety/environment/compatibility are valid properties; circulation moves indoor air while ventilation supplies/removes outdoor air; window AC is one casing while split AC has separate indoor/outdoor units and connecting pipes.
