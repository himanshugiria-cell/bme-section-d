Part A: syllabus-level clarification

1. Machining and CNC answer different questions

Machining answers: How is unwanted material removed? Drilling, turning and milling use a cutting tool. Other methods can remove material using electrical or thermal energy.

CNC = Computer Numerical Control. It answers: How are machine movements and operations controlled? A computer/controller follows programmed instructions. In CNC milling, the controller directs the cutter and workpiece movements, while the cutter physically removes material. A laser cutter can also follow CNC-controlled movements. CNC therefore names the control method, rather than one particular material-removal mechanism. NPTEL: CNC programming fundamentals, TWI: laser cutting.

Simple sequence: required drawing/model → program → controlled machine operation → inspection of the part.

Two useful benefits are repeatable programmed movements and the ability to produce complicated paths accurately. Correct tooling, setup and programming still matter: a wrong program can repeat the same error.

आसान भाषा में: CNC बताता है कि मशीन को कैसे चलाना है। Material कौन हटाता है—cutter, sparks या laser—यह अलग प्रश्न है।

2. Three newer machining examples

MethodWhat removes material?Main requirement and example
EDM — Electrical Discharge MachiningRepeated electrical sparks across a small gap between electrode and workpiece; local heating erodes small amounts of material.Ordinary EDM needs an electrically conductive workpiece. Example: making a cavity in a hardened-steel die.
ECM — Electrochemical MachiningControlled electrochemical dissolution: metal is removed by a reaction driven by electric current through an electrolyte.Conductive workpiece and a suitable electrolyte. Example: producing a shaped metal cavity or contour.
Laser machiningFocused light energy heats a small region. Material is melted and removed, or vaporised, depending on the process.Suitable laser/process for the material and thickness. Example: cutting an outline in thin metal sheet.

In EDM, the electrode does not need to push a cutting edge through the steel. A dielectric fluid occupies the machining region and helps the process. In ECM, the workpiece is the positive electrode, the tool is negative, and an electrolyte—an electrically conducting liquid—flows through the gap. ECM removes metal by dissolution, not sparks. In laser cutting, material removal can include an assist-gas jet that clears molten metal. These are different mechanisms. Makino: how EDM works, NPTEL: ECM, pp3–4, TWI: laser cutting.

Worked recognition example: A workshop wants a cavity in a hard, electrically conducting steel die. EDM is one possible route because sparks can remove conductive material without relying on a conventional cutting edge. This is an illustrative choice, not a rule that every hard part must be made by EDM.

Remember: sparks → EDM; current + electrolyte + dissolution → ECM; focused light → laser. A laser can also be used in some additive processes: identify whether material is being removed or added in the stated task.

3. Pattern allowances: three separate reasons

A pattern forms the mould cavity. Its geometry can differ from the final required part because manufacturing must allow for later changes.

AllowanceWhy it is providedWhat to remember
ShrinkageCompensates for dimensional contraction as the solid casting cools.Pattern dimensions are adjusted for contraction.
Machining/finishingLeaves material that can be removed later to obtain the required surface or size.Extra material is left on surfaces that will be machined.
Draft/taperHelps withdraw the pattern without damaging the sand cavity.A small taper is provided on withdrawal surfaces.

Draft is a taper, not simply extra thickness everywhere. Shrinkage allowance and the riser do different jobs: the pattern allowance adjusts dimensions; the riser supplies additional liquid metal as needed during solidification. NPTEL: pattern and shrinkage allowance, NPTEL: draft and machining allowances.

Recognition example: A vertical pattern wall rubs against and damages the sand when lifted. The relevant feature is draft allowance. A casting needs a smooth finished bearing surface after casting: leave machining allowance on that surface.

4. Moulding sand: ingredients and properties are different

An ingredient is something put into the mixture. A property describes how that mixture behaves.

IngredientJob in the mixture
Sand grainsForm the main body of the mould.
Binder, such as clay in green sandHelps grains hold together.
Controlled moisture in green sandHelps the clay-bonded mixture become workable and develop bonding.
Selected additivesAdjust properties such as finish or collapsibility.

Different moulding-sand systems use different binders; moisture is not a universal requirement for every system. “Green sand” means a moist moulding mixture, not green-coloured sand. NPTEL: green-sand moulding.

Five important properties: strength keeps the cavity intact; permeability lets gases pass through; refractoriness resists the heat of molten metal; plasticity helps the mixture take the required shape; collapsibility lets it yield/break away after the metal solidifies. Good sand balances these needs. Very strong sand is not automatically the best sand. Lecture 6 notes: moulding sand and furnaces, NPTEL: sand-property check.

Worked recognition example: A mould keeps its cavity shape, but gases become trapped. The property to examine is permeability, not simply strength. The ingredient helping grains stick is the binder. This distinction supports revision-quiz Question 10 and Assignment 2 Question 2.

For a mould sketch, remember: cope = upper half; drag = lower half; cavity = space shaped by the pattern; sprue/runner = passages for metal flow; riser = reservoir feeding the casting; core = forms an internal hole/passage. Their jobs are distinct.

5. Additive manufacturing: process, benefits and limits

Additive manufacturing builds a part from digital model data by adding material, usually layer by layer. A familiar plastic-printing example is: model → divide into layers (slice) → deposit material → finish and inspect. An overhanging region may need temporary supports. NIST: what additive manufacturing is, NIST: AM postprocessing.

Useful applications: checking the shape of a prototype, making customised parts, and creating some complicated internal or lightweight forms. Advantages include design flexibility and the ability to change a design without making a new dedicated mould for each version. NIST: additive manufacturing/3D printing.

Limits: available materials and machine/build size constrain the job; printing can take time; surface finish or accuracy may need further work; properties depend on the material, process and build conditions. Supports, finishing and inspection can add cost. A printed part is not automatically suitable for carrying a load just because its shape looks correct. NIST: measurement and quality challenges.

Example: Print a plastic bracket model to check fit and hole locations. Before using it to support a real load, check the material and required strength. For a precise pocket removed from a metal block, CNC milling is a suitable classroom example. Process choice depends on the actual job.

6. Forming: identify the tool, force and movement

Forming reshapes solid metal by force. Heating may make deformation easier, but the workpiece is not poured as a liquid.

ProcessBasic workingFamiliar application
ForgingA hammer or press compresses the workpiece between tools; the metal deforms into the required shape.A forged spanner or crankshaft.
RollingRotating rolls grip and pass the metal through a gap, reducing thickness or changing section.Metal sheet.
ExtrusionA ram pushes a billet through a shaped die opening; the product takes that cross-section.A long channel or rod.
DrawingA pulling force draws a wire or rod through a die, reducing its cross-section.Thinner wire.

For example, a channel pushed through a die is extrusion; a wire pulled through a die is drawing. Name the physical action before recalling the process name. Lecture 7: metal forming.

7. Arc and gas welding: how a joint forms

In arc welding, a power source establishes an electric arc between the electrode and workpiece. The arc supplies heat that melts the joint edges and, where used, filler metal. The weld pool then solidifies to join the pieces.

In gas welding, fuel gas burns with oxygen at the torch. The flame heats and melts the joint edges; a separate filler rod may be added. The joint solidifies after the flame moves away. Oxygen supports combustion; it is not itself the fuel. Lecture 8: welding, brazing and soldering.

Joining methodWhat melts in these introductory examples?Example use
Fusion weldingBase-metal edges; filler may also melt.Fabricating a steel frame.
BrazingFiller with melting temperature above 450°C; base pieces remain solid.Joining a suitable tube assembly.
SolderingLower-temperature filler, below 450°C; base pieces remain solid.Making a PCB electrical connection.

These examples support Assignment 2 Questions 3–4. Joint choice depends on material, service conditions and technique, rather than the process name alone. TWI: joining-method comparison.

8. Pattern types — Assignment 2, Question 1

  • Single-piece pattern: one complete pattern; useful when its shape can be withdrawn easily from the mould.
  • Split/two-piece pattern: halves separate at the parting surface, with alignment features; one half forms the cope-side cavity and the other the drag-side cavity.
  • Match-plate pattern: pattern halves are mounted on opposite faces of a plate, helping consistent alignment and repeated mould production.

These are representative introductory types, not an exhaustive catalogue. Draw each arrangement and label the pattern, parting surface/plate and alignment. NPTEL: types of pattern.

9. Hot/cold working and recrystallisation — Assignment 2, Question 5

Cold working plastically reshapes metal below its recrystallisation temperature; hot working takes place above this temperature but below melting. The metal remains solid in both.

Recrystallisation forms new, relatively strain-free grains in previously deformed metal. Its temperature depends on the material and processing conditions; there is no one value for all metals. It is not the melting point.

Cold working — typical tendencyHot working — typical tendency
Higher forming force; strain hardening can occur.Lower forming force and larger deformation may be possible.
Often better finish and dimensional control.Heating is needed; oxidation/scale can affect finish.

“Cold” does not always mean room temperature. Classify working relative to recrystallisation, rather than by whether the piece feels warm. NPTEL: cold and hot working.

10. Extrusion variants — Assignment 2, Question 6

Extrusion forces a billet through a die opening to produce a cross-section.

  • Direct/forward: product moves in the same direction as the ram. Billet movement along the container wall creates friction.
  • Indirect/backward: the die moves into a billet through a hollow ram; product flows opposite to the ram's travel. Billet–container sliding is reduced.
  • By temperature: hot and cold extrusion are a different classification basis. A process can therefore be described by both its flow arrangement and its working temperature.

Long metal channels and rods are familiar examples. Tubes need tooling that forms the hollow section. Other variants include hydrostatic extrusion, using pressurised fluid, and impact extrusion, using a rapid punch stroke; these names are additional examples, not required design calculations. NPTEL: extrusion principles and variants.

Figure. The classification compares ram travel with product flow. Hot/cold is a separate temperature classification.

These imperfections can affect joint performance. This is a recognition list, not a procedure for deciding whether a particular weld is acceptable. TWI: realistic weld imperfections, TWI: porosity, AWS: undercut and weld edges.

Joining clarification for Question 4: In the fusion-welding examples taught, base edges melt and join. Brazing and soldering melt a separate filler while the base pieces stay solid. Brazing uses filler melting temperatures above 450°C; soldering uses temperatures below 450°C. Welding also includes solid-state methods, so “all welding melts the base metal” is too broad. Revise arc/gas heat sources and the joining-method applications from Lecture 8 alongside this supplement. TWI: welding, brazing and soldering, TWI: solid-state welding.

Revision questions and answers

Try the questions before reading the answers. These are learning checks, not an announcement of examination questions.

  1. A programmed milling machine removes a pocket. Which term describes control, and what removes material? CNC describes control; the milling cutter removes material.
  2. Match sparks, electrolyte-driven dissolution and focused light to processes. EDM, ECM and laser machining, respectively.
  3. Why is ordinary EDM unsuitable for a nonconducting plastic workpiece? Its spark-removal process needs an electrically conductive workpiece.
  4. A pattern damages the cavity when withdrawn. Which allowance helps? What allowance leaves material for finishing? Draft/taper; machining allowance.
  5. Does binder mean permeability? No. Binder is an ingredient holding grains together; permeability is the mixture's ability to pass gases.
  6. A printed part fits perfectly. Is that enough to approve it for supporting a load? No. Check its material, properties, dimensions and suitability for the actual load; shape/fit alone is insufficient.
  7. How do direct and indirect extrusion differ? Product flow follows the ram in direct extrusion and opposes it in indirect extrusion. Hot/cold describes the temperature basis, not that flow direction.
  8. Is metal heated below melting necessarily hot-worked? What is weld porosity? No: hot working is classified relative to recrystallisation. Weld porosity consists of trapped gas cavities.

Final study check: Can you explain the action, label the important parts, name an example and distinguish a similar process? Use the original notes, current slides and these additions together. Ask about any step that is still unclear.
